Everything stays on this computer

  • Your deployments (the air conditioners, their tokens, your Wi-Fi password, the API key) are kept by the helper on this computer, in its own folder, and nowhere else.
  • This page can talk to the helper on this computer (127.0.0.1) and to nothing else. Its security policy forbids any other connection, and your browser enforces that.
  • The helper goes on the internet for two things only: to download μCore's source and what builds it (GitHub, static.rust-lang.org, and crates.io for the Rust libraries; aspic for breeze-mock, if you make simulated air conditioners), and, only when you fetch tokens, to Midea's cloud, with your own Midea account. That is the one thing that cannot be done on this computer: only Midea hands out the air conditioners' tokens.
  • Everything the helper connects to is listed here as it happens.

The helper is not running

This page only shows what the deployer's helper on your computer does, and the helper opens it for you with the link it needs. To start the helper:

  • Linux or macOS, in a terminal: curl -fsSL https://aspic.salataputarica.hr.eu.org/ucore-deploy/run.sh | sh
  • Windows, in PowerShell: irm https://aspic.salataputarica.hr.eu.org/ucore-deploy/run.ps1 | iex
  • From a checkout of μCore: cargo run --release in its deploy folder.

Each checks the helper against its SHA-256 before running it. If the helper is already running, open the link it printed.

This copy of the page comes from aspic, so it reaches the helper only if your browser lets it connect to apps on this computer. If your browser asked and you said no, change that in the site's settings (the icon beside the address) and reload. If your browser refuses outright, start the helper without --aspic: it then opens the same page itself.

    From a Breeze Core you already have

    Pick that server's config.json. It is read here, in your browser, and handed to the helper on this computer; it goes nowhere else.

    From Midea's cloud (with breeze-token)

    This is the one step that uses the internet with your details. The helper finds the air conditioners on your network, asks Midea's clouds which one has your account (by name only, no password), then signs in to that one with your password, once, to fetch each unit's token. It checks every token on its unit before keeping it. The password is not stored and not logged.

    
    
    

    
    
      Simulated ones, to try μCore without any (breeze-mock)
      
    breeze-mock plays air conditioners on their real network protocol, here on this computer, beside the board in QEMU. They start and stop with the board, and only this computer can reach them. Making them again replaces the simulated ones; real units stay. The first time, the helper downloads breeze-mock from aspic and checks it.
    
      
    Simulated units are for a board in QEMU: a real board would need them on your network, which the deployer does not do yet.
